RobustRLlib A Unified Library and Benchmark for Robust Reinforcement Learning Algorithms

Sixteen robust online, offline and safe RL methods behind one interface, evaluated with six standard algorithms under six shift sources and five modes, from standard control to humanoid and vision–language–action tasks.

Abstract

Robust reinforcement learning (RL) is critical for reliable real-world deployment, where policies may fail under diverse shifts and disruptions. Existing robust RL algorithms offer a rich set of mechanisms and principles, yet two major barriers limit their broader potential: limited algorithm reusability and an unclear empirical capability boundary. We introduce RobustRLlib, an algorithm-centric library and benchmark that addresses both challenges. It integrates 16 representative robust online, offline, and safe RL methods under a unified interface for substantially improved reusability, together with an enhanced evaluation platform spanning diverse real-world shifts and advanced humanoid and vision–language–action tasks. Systematic evaluation over 273 perturbation configurations provides an up-to-date capability map of current robust RL algorithms, revealing where they remain effective and where substantial robustness gaps persist. As an open-source, user-friendly platform, RobustRLlib offers a reproducible and extensible codebase for applying existing methods to new problems and developing new robust RL algorithms.

Standard · 1 of 6

IQL

Implicit Q-Learning

Standard reference

Expectile-regression value learning that never queries out-of-distribution actions.

IQL avoids querying out-of-distribution actions altogether. A value function is fitted to the critic by expectile regression, the twin critics bootstrap from that value function, and the actor is extracted by advantage-weighted regression.

Original paperKostrikov et al. Offline Reinforcement Learning with Implicit Q-Learning. arXiv:2110.06169, 2021.

TD3+BC

TD3 with Behaviour Cloning

Standard reference

TD3 with a behaviour-cloning term balanced against the critic.

TD3+BC adds a behaviour-cloning term to the TD3 actor loss and normalises states. The term is balanced against the critic by dividing by the mean absolute Q-value over the batch.

Original paperFujimoto and Gu A Minimalist Approach to Offline Reinforcement Learning. NeurIPS, 2021.

MOPO

Model-based Offline Policy Optimization

Standard referenceBase · SAC

SAC on real and model data, with rollout rewards penalised by model uncertainty.

MOPO learns an ensemble dynamics model, branches short rollouts from dataset states, and penalises the rollout reward by the model's predicted uncertainty before running SAC on the mixture of real and model data.

Original paperYu et al. MOPO: Model-based Offline Policy Optimization. NeurIPS, 2020.

RARL

Robust Adversarial Reinforcement Learning

Environment-centricBase · PPO / TRPOClaims · dynamic shift

A two-player zero-sum game against an adversary that applies external forces.

RARL casts robustness as a two-player zero-sum game. An adversary applies bounded external forces to the robot and is trained to minimise the protagonist's return, and the two are updated in alternating blocks.

What the paper concludes

  1. Robust RL helps at every severity, but both degrade as shifts intensify.
  2. Dynamics-aware mechanisms are stronger under dynamics shifts.
  3. Claimed channels do not fully predict empirical strengths.
  4. Online robust methods generally yield larger gains over their base learners than offline methods.
  5. Dynamics are the common first shift, but the second varies.
  6. Robustness transfers return but not constraint satisfaction; without the second, a transfer is not deployable.
  7. Sensitivity differs sharply across shift sources, and the semantic shift affects more in VLA task.

Quick start

Declare a shift, train, evaluate.

Declare a shifted environment

# A deployment condition is an ordered list of shifts. The list order is the stacking order.
from robustrllib import make_robust, ShiftSpec

env = make_robust("Hopper-v5", shifts=[
    # theta^p — gravity scaled to 1.3 of its nominal value
    ShiftSpec("dynamics",    "scale", {"param": "gravity", "factor": 1.3}),
    # theta^o — Gaussian noise on the observation
    ShiftSpec("observation", "gauss", {"sigma": 0.05}),
], seed=0)                                  # a standard Gymnasium environment

Train a method and evaluate it under shift

# Train on the nominal task with the method's native recipe.
python baselines/rorl/train_rorl.py \
    -c robustrllib/configs/experiment/rorl_hopper.yaml --seed 0

# Freeze the last checkpoint and evaluate it on the shared perturbation grid:
# 20 episodes per condition, the same episodes for every method, unclipped normalized score.
python baselines/eval_final.py \
    -c robustrllib/configs/experiment/rorl_hopper.yaml \
    --run-dir runs/rorl/hopper_medium_seed0
